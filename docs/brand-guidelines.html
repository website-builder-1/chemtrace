Chemtraceit · Introduction01 / 09

A synthesis plan you can cost, source and defend.

Chemtraceit takes a molecule and returns ranked synthesis routes, the suppliers behind every starting material, and the regulatory and geopolitical risk each route carries.

The brand serves people who read data for a living. Chemists check structures, procurement checks prices, compliance checks the rules. Everything we make should hold up to that kind of reading: precise, plain, and backed by a figure or a source.

The mark is called Atlas. A molecular route crosses a folded map, because a synthesis plan is also a supply map. The chemistry and the geography are one decision, and the brand keeps them in one picture.

Principle 1

Precise

Real units, real structures, real currencies. A number is never decoration.

Principle 2

Plain

We describe what the tool does in the words a chemist would use at the bench.

Principle 3

Sourced

Every risk flag names its reason. If we estimate, we say it is an estimate.

Chemtraceit · Motion05 / 09

Motion

Motion shows the system working: a route being traced, a result arriving. It never performs for its own sake.

Arrive

Results, panels and routes entering view.

320ms · cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)

Move

Reordering routes, switching tabs, toggles.

200ms · cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)

Control

Hover, press, checkbox and focus states.

160ms · ease-out

Signature: the route trace

When routes load, the line draws from starting material to target and each step lands in order. This is the one moment where motion is allowed to be noticed.

Nothing bounces. Curves settle without overshoot. A lab instrument does not wobble.

One thing at a time. If two elements move together they share a curve and a duration.

Numbers swap, they don't slide. A price or score fades through to its new value so it never reads as two numbers.

Over 400ms is a stall. The route trace is the only exception. Reduced motion settings turn all of it off.

Chemtraceit · Voice and tone06 / 09

Voice and tone

We sound like a colleague who has already checked the supplier list: specific, calm, and clear about what we know and what we are estimating.

Say what the chemist would say

Name the reagent, the step, the country. "Step 2 needs a Schedule 1 precursor" beats "regulatory considerations apply".

Give the reason

Every flag says why it was raised and where the rule comes from. A score without a reason is a guess.

Be plain about limits

When something is an estimate or still in development, we say so in the same sentence. No asterisks.

DO

Route B relies on one supplier region for step 2.

DON'T

Unlock unprecedented visibility into your global supply chain.

DO

Restricted for UK buyers. Home Office licence required for this precursor.

DON'T

Some compliance issues may apply to this route.

DO

Estimated cost for 5 g: £412.60. Freight is not included yet.

DON'T

Get instant, accurate pricing for any molecule.

DO

No suppliers found for this building block. Try a different disconnection.

DON'T

Oops! Something went wrong.

SpellingBritish English: synthesise, colour, licence (noun).
CurrencyGBP first, then USD and EUR. Symbol before the figure.
PunctuationCommas and full stops. No em dashes, no exclamation marks.
ChemistrySMILES and CAS numbers in mono. Never restyle or hyphenate them.
ClaimsNo "first", "only" or "guaranteed". Describe what the tool does.
ButtonsSentence case, verbs that state the result: View invoice, Compare routes. Downloads start with ↓.

Chemtraceit · Imagery07 / 09

Imagery

Our graphics come from two real sources: reaction schemes and maps. Use them as line work on flat ground, never as glowing 3D molecules.

Contour fieldBackground texture for covers and dark panels. Low contrast only.
Reaction schemeSkeletal structures, conditions in mono. Ink lines, teal arrows.
Supply lanesSupplier to buyer as dashed lanes on a grid. Colour only when a lane carries a status.

Photography: use

  • Real bench work: glassware, balances, fume hoods, labelled reagent bottles
  • Natural or overhead lab light, slightly warm
  • Hands and process, not posed portraits

Photography: avoid

  • Blue glowing molecules, floating hexagons, DNA helices
  • Globes with light trails or "network" overlays
  • Lab coats posing at empty screens
